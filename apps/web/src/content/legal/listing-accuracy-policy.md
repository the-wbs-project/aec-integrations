AEC Integrations catalogs software products, vendors, and the integrations between them so that architecture, engineering, and construction teams can find and compare them. This policy explains where listing data comes from, who maintains an integration, how to ask for a correction, and the limits of what we will change. It works together with our [Terms of Service](/legal/terms). The contest procedure and its time limits apply to companies that have claimed a listing. Anyone else may use the correction request below; it is a process we offer, not a term we impose.

## Where listing data comes from

Listings are compiled from **publicly available information**, including vendor websites, public product and API documentation, public integration directories and marketplaces, and similar sources, together with information sent to us by people who use the service and, where applicable, confirmed by vendors. We curate this into an AEC-native taxonomy and keep it as current as we reasonably can.

A listing is a factual catalog entry, not an advertisement. We do not sell listings or ranking position, and inclusion does not imply any relationship with, or endorsement by, the vendor.

## Who maintains an integration

An integration is a listing of how two products connect. It belongs to the vendor that built and offers it, which the listing names on its "Offered by" line, as recorded from that vendor's own documentation. We record integrations from public sources to start the catalog. The vendor named as the owner may then claim the integration through its vendor account. Once it does, the owner maintains the integration's details, its edits are published without review by us, and our own catalog updates no longer change that integration, except a correction under Requesting a correction below. A vendor may also add an integration of its own, which it maintains from the start. An integration delivered through a third-party connector product can be claimed, edited and contested in the same way by its owner. A correction request applies to it at any time.

An owner may **retire** an integration it has claimed. A retired integration is taken off the public site but is not deleted, and the owner may restore it. We may also retire an integration a vendor maintains, where this policy lets us remove a listing: to correct an error, or to enforce our terms. Only we may restore an integration we retired.

## We aim for accuracy, but we don't guarantee it

We work to keep listings accurate and up to date, but public sources change and may themselves be incomplete or wrong. We do not warrant that any listing is complete, current, or error-free, and listings may lag behind a product's actual state. If something looks wrong, please tell us.

## Requesting a correction

Anyone, including a vendor, can ask us to correct a factual error in a listing. Email [support@aecintegrations.com](mailto:support@aecintegrations.com) (or use the correction option in the service) and include:

- a link to the listing;
- what specifically is inaccurate; and
- the corrected information, with a public source we can verify.

We review every request, verify it against public sources, and update the listing where the correction is supported. We aim to respond promptly. We may decline or defer a request we cannot verify, and we decide at our discretion how a correction is reflected. A correction request needs a public source we can verify. It is not a second route for a declined contest.

If a listing a vendor maintains is inaccurate, we ask the vendor to correct it. Where you send us, by email to the address below or through the correction option in the service, a public source that shows a value is wrong, we hide or flag that value within two business days of receiving it. Hiding is an interim step while we verify, not a finding that the value is false. If our review confirms the error against that source, we correct it within five business days, or the vendor does, and sooner where verification is immediate. Where a value is genuinely disputed, we tell you within five business days what further verification we need and when we expect to decide. Business days are Monday to Friday, excluding US federal holidays. A correction we make ourselves is marked as ours, with its source and date. We tell the vendor what we changed and why, and we keep a record of when we received the request, what we verified, when we hid the value and what we decided. A corrected detail stays as we set it until we review it again, and the vendor can dispute it by writing to us at [support@aecintegrations.com](mailto:support@aecintegrations.com).

## Contesting an integration detail

A vendor whose product is at one end of an integration, and which does not own that integration, has a second route: a **contest**, sent from its vendor account. A contest names one detail of the integration, the value the vendor believes is correct, and the reason.

Who decides a contest:

- if the owner has claimed the integration, **the owner** decides;
- if the integration has not been claimed, **we** decide; and
- a contest about **who owns** the integration always comes to us, even after a claim.

A contest is decided by whoever was deciding when it was sent, even if the integration is claimed later. While a contest is open, the listing continues to show the value on record and does not indicate that a contest exists.

A contest ends in one of three ways:

- **Accepted.** The proposed value replaces the value on record. Where we accept a contest on an integration nobody has claimed, the change is generally published with our next catalog update.
- **Declined.** The decider does not agree with the proposed value. The value on record stays, the contest is closed, and the vendor that sent it is told, together with any note the decider added. Where the owner declined it, the vendor may ask us to review the decision, as described below.
- **Withdrawn.** The vendor that sent a contest may withdraw it while it is open. An open contest is also closed as withdrawn if the owner or we retire the integration, and restoring the integration does not reopen it.

### Asking us to review an owner's decision

Where the owner declined a contest, the vendor that sent it may ask us to review that decision within 30 days of the decline. Where the owner has not answered a contest for 30 days, we treat it as declined, and the vendor may ask us to review it within the following 30 days. The request gives the vendor's reasons and may include up to three links. The owner may reply once, within 14 days. We give our view after the owner replies or after those 14 days, whichever comes first. We give it sooner only where the owner tells us it will not reply, or where the value on record is causing harm that cannot wait, and in that case we say so in our view.

We then say which side we agree with, and give our reasons to both vendors. **Our view is advice.** We do not change the integration, because the owner maintains its details. The value on record stays unless the owner changes it. If our review shows the value on record is a factual error we can verify against a public source, we may correct it under Requesting a correction above. Where we agree with the owner, the vendor that asked may not contest the same detail of that integration again for 90 days, unless the value on record changes in the meantime. The vendor that asked may withdraw its request while we are considering it, and it may not ask again about the same contest.

We do not publish anything about a review: not that one was asked for, and not our view.

A contest is separate from a correction request. Anyone may still ask us for a correction under the section above.

## What we will and will not change

We will:

- **correct factual errors**: for example a wrong vendor name, miscategorized product, broken or outdated capability, or an integration listed in error; and
- **remove listings for defunct companies or discontinued products**: where a vendor or product no longer exists or is no longer offered, for example the company has dissolved or the product's own site says it is discontinued.

We will **not**:

- **remove an accurate listing simply because a vendor asks us to.** AEC Integrations is an independent directory built from public information; a vendor's preference not to be listed is not, on its own, a reason to remove an accurate entry. We remove a listing only to correct an error, reflect that a company or product is defunct, comply with a legal obligation, or enforce our terms.

This independence is deliberate: it is what lets the directory stay useful and trustworthy rather than reflecting only what vendors want shown.

When we remove a listing, we hide it. A hidden listing is taken off the public site and out of search, but its data is kept so it can be restored if the reason for hiding it turns out to be wrong. Hiding is not deletion. We hide a listing, or the contested part of it, while we verify a complaint. We obey a valid court order or lawful government process directed to us. A request to remove a name is answered by this policy. A complaint that a logo or screenshot infringes copyright goes through the copyright notice process in our Terms of Service. A complaint that a logo is the complainant's trademark is answered by this policy, and we remove a logo we sourced ourselves at once when its owner objects. We tell the vendor of a claimed listing when we hide it and why, and it may dispute the decision by writing to us.

## Reviews are separate

This policy covers the **accuracy of catalog listings**. Concerns about a **review**, for example that it is fake, defamatory, or names an individual, are handled under our [Review Guidelines](/legal/review-guidelines), not here.
