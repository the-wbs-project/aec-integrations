Every pair of products that work together has one integration page. Its heading reads "How (one product) and (the other) exchange data". You reach it from the Integrations section of either product's page.

If the two products connect in more than one way, each way has its own card on the same page.

## The top of the page

**The two products.** They sit side by side, each with its vendor's name. A product shows its rating and review count once it has enough reviews to show a rating. Choose a product to open its page. Directions on this page are always told from the product on the left.

**Who keeps the page up to date.** The "About this listing" panel, after the cards, says so in its first line. "Vendor maintained" means a company at one end has acted on the record, for example by claiming it or confirming its data. It does not tell you who offers the integration. The "Offered by" line on each card does that. Every integration page has this panel. "AEC Integrations maintained" means we keep the page up to date. On a narrow screen the panel is closed until you open it.

**Basic and Detailed.** When a page has more detail to show, two buttons switch between views. Detailed is the default. It lists each type of data with its direction and agreement label. Basic hides those lists and keeps each card's summary. The page remembers your choice in this browser.

## Types of data shared

Under the products, a heading counts the types of data that move between them, such as "4 types of data shared". It counts each type once, however many cards it appears on. A type of data that a company says is not accurate, and nobody has confirmed, is not counted.

If nothing is listed yet, the heading reads "We haven’t listed what’s shared yet". If we know the direction but not the types of data, it reads "We haven’t listed the types of data yet". If every type of data listed is one a company says is not accurate, it reads "No type of data is confirmed".

## Each card

A card is one way the two products connect.

**The heading** is the integration's name. When it has no name, the heading is its kind, such as API or Native. [How listings are classified](/docs/getting-started/taxonomy) lists the kinds.

**The chips** beside the heading say which way data moves, from the left-hand product's side:

- **Sends to**, then the other product's name: it sends data to the other product.
- **Receives from**, then the other product's name: it receives data from the other product.
- **Syncs both ways**: data moves both ways.

When a page has more than one card, a chip such as "3 types of data" says how many each card covers.

**A Sync Service card.** A Sync Service is a product that runs a finished sync between two other products. Its card says who offers it, and that the two products' vendors do not offer or endorse it. Once the Sync Service has five reviews, its card also shows its own rating and review count, as the two products show theirs.

**At a glance** collects the facts we have about this integration. A fact we do not have is left out.

- **Price**: how it is priced, as recorded. Where the company that offers it has a pricing page, the price links to it, or the link reads "See pricing".
- **Release stage**: how far along it is, as recorded.
- **How you get it**: the kind of integration, then "Offered by" and the company that offers it. That is the company you get the integration from. "Through" names a connector product, when the data passes through one. "Added by the vendor" means a vendor added it to the directory itself. We did not research it.
- **Last checked**: the date the integration was last checked, where there is one. Many integrations have no date yet.

**The data lists** appear in the Detailed view. They group the types of data by direction, under headings such as "Sends to (other product)", "Receives from (other product)" and "Syncs both ways". Each row names one type of data and carries an agreement label:

- Listed by AEC Integrations
- A company says no
- Confirmed by (a company)
- Confirmed by both companies
- Companies disagree

[Agreement states](/docs/trust/agreement-states) explains each one and what to do with it.

**The i button** at the end of a row opens **Sources**. It lists everyone who has answered for that type of data, for example "(Company) confirms this" or "(Company) says this is not accurate". A closing line says what is missing, such as a company that has not answered yet. A company's reasons are never shown on the public page.

**The links** at the foot of a card go to listings and documentation outside AEC Integrations. A link named after a product, such as "(Product) documentation", was added by that product's company, and it reads "Link from (company)". "View listing" and "Documentation" are links we recorded.

## About this listing

After the cards, one panel says who wrote what on the page.

- **"Maintained by (company) · updated (date)"** means that company wrote the details on a card and last changed them on that date. They publish without review by us, and the line does not mean we checked them. When the page has more than one card, the line names the card.
- **"(Field): Corrected by AEC Integrations on (date)"** names a value we corrected, with a "Source" link when we recorded one.
- **"(Field): Removed by AEC Integrations on (date)"** names a value we removed. The value no longer shows on the card.
- A count says how many values on the page are under review. Each one reads "Under review" where its value would be.

A card with no line in this panel has no company edit behind it.

## When there is nothing listed

If no integration is recorded between two products, the page says "We have no integration between (one product) and (the other) on record." Under it: "Not on record means we have not found one. It is not proof that none exists." Below that, the page shows the data each product exchanges with its other integrations on record, in three groups: only the first product, both, only the second. Then it lists the closest matches: products that the product you keep has an integration on record with, and that share a category with the other product, A to Z by name. You can switch to the other product's integrations, search by product or vendor name, turn categories off, or pick data. The page then sets the two products side by side. It shows how many integrations each has on record. When either product has reviews, it also shows each one's review count and ratings. A product shows ratings once it has five reviews. Where we list them, it shows who each product is for. Last, it links each vendor's own integrations page and API documentation, where we have the link on record. Those pages are the vendor's words, not ours. When a product has integrations on record, the page also links that product's list here. An API is not an integration. If you know of one, choose "It does exist" under the statement and tell us where you saw it. See [Requests and corrections](/docs/reviewers/requests-and-corrections).

## On a product's page

A product's Integrations section lists its integrations in groups. Integrations delivered through a connector are grouped under "Via" and the connector's name, or "Via a connector". The rest sit under "Direct integrations".

A row does not say who maintains it. Open the integration page: its "About this listing" panel does.

Some products also show a line such as "3 more pairs reachable via connectors". That is a count only. Those pairs have no integration page yet.
