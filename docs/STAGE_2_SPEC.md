## 1. Overview

Stage 1 shipped a **public, read-only** directory: AECi curates every product, vendor, integration, and claim; the public reads. Stage 1.5 added the integration claim/attestation spine but kept it **AECi-seeded** — every claim renders **"Unverified"** because no vendor can yet attest.

**Stage 2 is where the vendors log in.** The dividing line is exactly the one drawn in `STAGE_1_5_SPEC.md` §1.1: *anything that requires a vendor to authenticate and assert something about their own product is Stage 2.* That single capability — the **vendor portal** — unlocks the other two pillars (paid tiers layered on verified vendor accounts; real-time updates so vendor edits reflect live).

**Primary new user:** a **vendor** (software publisher) who — through a **paid, AECi-verified account** (§8.1(3)) — wants to claim their product, correct its data, attest to its integrations, and unlock a richer profile.

**The trust invariant is unchanged and non-negotiable:** **no pay-for-placement.** Paid tiers affect *profile richness and portal capability only, never ranking position* (`STAGE_1_SPEC.md` §1 principles; `CLAUDE.md` constraints). Search stays purely algorithmic.

**Out of scope for Stage 2 (still later stages):** rich media profiles (Stage 4), trust scoring beyond basic anti-abuse (Stage 3), a public write API product.

## 2. Scope pillars

The three §18 pillars, plus the two carried-over surfaces (integration attestations, dark theme) that only Stage 2 can complete. Each maps to a Linear epic (§7).

### 2.1 Vendor Portal & Self-Serve Claiming — *the anchor*

> **Build contract:** this pillar is decomposed and specified in **`docs/STAGE_2_VENDOR_PORTAL_SPEC.md`** (the AECI-513 epic). The subsections below remain the scope outline; that doc is what each sub-issue anchors to.

The foundation everything else layers on. A vendor authenticates, proves association with a vendor record, and gains scoped write access to their own product/vendor data.

- **Claim flow — concierge / manual at launch (§8.1).** A vendor requests ownership of a `vendors` row (and its products). Builds on the Stage 1 Phase 6 request pipeline (`docs/STAGE_1_PHASE_6_SPEC.md`) and the domain-match hint, but escalates it from a correction request to a **verified account grant that AECi approves by hand**: on approval, a `profiles.role = 'vendor_admin'` row is linked to the `vendors` row via `profiles.vendor_id`, and `vendors.verified` flips true. **Verification is a paid gate** (§8.1(3), with the connector carve-out in §8.8 and its return side in §8.9 — a connector-vendor claim is **routed to the partnership track, never granted or declined**; procedure in `STAGE_2_VENDOR_PORTAL_SPEC.md` §5.2) arranged by **offline invoice/PO** (§8.1(5)) — the same admin action records the payment arrangement and toggles entitlement. *(As built — AECI-519: the grant mechanics shipped as `PATCH /api/admin/claims/:id`; see `STAGE_2_VENDOR_PORTAL_SPEC.md` §3 / §3.1.)*
- **Admin claim-review surface — reviewer-assisted verification (§8.1(1)).** The AECi-facing review UI presents **verification signals** to the reviewer: email-domain match against the vendor's known domain(s), an email→LinkedIn/person lookup, and any other enrichment that helps confirm the claimant. No auto-grant — a human decides on the assembled evidence.
- **Provisioning is an app-layer seam.** There is no cross-system FK between Supabase `auth.users` and D1 `profiles` (AECI-254) — the same seam the admin role uses. Vendor grants are written app-side, audited, and reversible.
- **`/api/vendor/*` endpoint surface.** Mirrors `/api/admin/*` (§18's observation still holds), scoped by `vendor_id` in the Worker + Drizzle query, **not** by RLS (§4). Every write emits its `audit_log` row in the same `db.batch()`.
- **Vendor portal UI.** Edit product/vendor content (name, description, links, taxonomy within guard-rails), see claim/correction status, manage the verified badge. **Multi-seat** (§8.1(2)) — several admins per vendor. The FIRST seat is granted through the review above; since AECI-664 an **owner** seat can invite colleagues self-serve at **any** email address (`STAGE_2_VENDOR_PORTAL_SPEC.md` §11a — the original same-domain restriction was removed 2026-08-26, §11a.3), and someone with no owner to ask still falls through to that review. Flat in data capability, not in seat management: `profiles.seat_owner` gates invite/remove alone.
- **Verified-badge activation.** `vendors.verified` already exists and is indexed (§3); Stage 2 lights it up in the UI and the trust surface.
- **Moderation escalation.** `profiles.banned_at` / `ban_reason` (§3) gate vendor write access; abuse of the portal is a ban path.

### 2.2 Paid Tiers & Entitlements — *no pay-for-placement*

> **Build contract:** this pillar is decomposed and specified in **`docs/STAGE_2_PAID_TIERS_SPEC.md`** (the AECI-515 epic). The subsections below remain the scope outline; that doc is what each sub-issue anchors to.

Monetization layered on verified vendor accounts. **Ranking is never for sale;** **vendors pay, always** (§8.1(4)) — and the vendor who pays is the **endpoint** vendor (§8.8).

- **Tier model (§8.1(3)).** The free/default state is the **unclaimed, AECi-curated baseline** (renders "Unverified"). **Verified is a paid gate** — a modest entry fee unlocks claim, data correction, integration attestation, the verified badge, richer profile fields, vendor analytics, and version-diff depth; the **connector** surface is not invoiced (§8.8). Entitlements gate **richness + capability only — never ranking, placement, or badge trust.** Any richness ladder above the entry Verified fee is **still open** (§8.2).
- **Billing — offline invoicing / PO at launch (§8.1(5)).** No payment-provider integration in the first slice; larger vendors pay by PO/invoice, not a credit card. The system needs an **admin-toggled entitlement/verification state** + basic invoice/arrangement tracking (notices/receipts over Resend — §4). Automated billing (Stripe or similar) and self-serve card are **deferred** — start with the minimum.
- **Entitlement enforcement.** A single entitlement gate consulted by `/api/vendor/*` and the render path; entitlements are **data, not code branches scattered across the app**, and the flag is payer-model-agnostic (§8.1(4)).
- **Relates to** the Stage 1.5 carve-out AECI-304 (paywalled integration/version-diff depth) — the first concrete paid capability. Hard invariant: the *latest-version* view **and** the latest conflict / single-source state are always free and full-fidelity to readers; only the historical *diff depth* is gateable; one-sided (paid-vs-unpaid) states are visibly labeled (this also mitigates the §8.1(3) attestation-bias risk).

### 2.3 Real-Time / Live Portal

> **Build contract:** this pillar is decomposed and specified in **`docs/STAGE_2_REALTIME_SPEC.md`** (the AECI-516 epic), over the transport decision recorded as **ADR 0023** and §8.6 below. The subsection below remains the scope outline; that doc is what each sub-issue anchors to.

**The transport question this section carried is closed.** §18 said "WebSockets/SSE for the portal", and §4(5) below translated that to Durable Objects — but neither was a decision, and §8.2 deferred the real one to this epic. Resolved 2026-08-19: **the portal goes live through scoped client revalidation over a cheap per-vendor freshness cursor. Durable-Object WebSockets and SSE are not adopted** — a dated decision with a named re-open trigger (ADR 0023), not a permanent no. The deciding evidence is that **no event that can change a vendor's portal state is sub-second**: the vendor's own edit needs zero transport (the write's echo already carries post-edit state), two of the six event classes are produced by a **daily cron**, and the only real deadline is the concierge entitlement toggle at ≤ ~1 min.

This is *not* "no live updates" — both bullets ship:

- **Live vendor edits.** A vendor's change reflects without a full reload: a shared `VendorPortalStore` with optimistic local mutation and server reconciliation for the **toggle-shaped** writes (assert / deny / retract), plus cross-section revalidation so a write in one section moves the counts in another. Form-shaped writes (profile, product) deliberately keep their pessimistic echo-reconcile — showing "Saved" before it saved is a worse lie than a 300 ms wait. A section holding unsaved changes is **never clobbered** by a background refresh.
- **Notification delivery.** New §2.4 detector nudges (`silent-counterparty`, `open-conflict`, `stale-version`, `aeci-denied`) appear in-portal within one poll interval, without a reload, alongside — not instead of — the Resend email that stays the primary channel. They surface as a **session-scoped count, never a banner**: the rows are a historical record of an email sent, so a three-week-old "Vendors disagree" row rendered as current state would contradict a lane that now reads `confirmed`.
- **The mechanism.** A new read-only `GET /api/vendor/updates` returns a **per-scope** revision map (`profile` · `entitlement` · `products` · `integrations` · `notifications` · `requests`) as six SELECTs in one `db.batch`, polled at **20 s focused / 60 s unfocused / paused hidden**; the client refetches only the scopes that moved. **No migration, no new binding, no queue, no `Cache-Tag`** — `/api/vendor/*` is `private, no-store` and never edge-cached.

> **Built out 2026-08-19 (AECI-626…632).** All seven sub-issues shipped, and the "no migration, no binding, no queue, no `Cache-Tag`" promise held literally — nothing under `apps/api/migrations/` or `apps/web/src/server/` was touched. The server side is one route; the client side is five files under `apps/web/src/app/vendor/`. Six pre-build statements diverged from what shipped and are corrected in `STAGE_2_REALTIME_SPEC.md` (indexed in its §1.5); the two most likely to mislead a reader of *this* section are that the live region is fed by a dedicated announcer service rather than by the store (§8.6(7) below), and that the Integrations tab gates on `vendors.verified` rather than a capability — because that is the field the **API** gates on, and the client's rule is to read whatever the API asserts on.

### 2.4 Integration Attestations & Conflict — *activating the dormant 1.5 spine*

> **Build contract:** this pillar is decomposed and specified in **`docs/STAGE_2_ATTESTATIONS_SPEC.md`** (the AECI-514 epic). The subsection below remains the scope outline; that doc is what each sub-issue anchors to.

Stage 1.5 shipped the claim spine with **dormant** `vendor_a`/`vendor_b` attestation sources and additive `introduced_at`/`deprecated_at` version stamps (§3). Stage 2 makes them live. These are **already tracked** as Stage 1.5 carve-outs and become the sub-issues of this epic:

- **Vendor attestation authoring** (AECI-301) — **shipped 2026-08-17; `STAGE_2_ATTESTATIONS_SPEC.md` §5 + §5.4.** Vendors assert/deny claims **and create their own**, producing real `vendor_a`/`vendor_b` attestations; `computeAgreement` (already unit-tested, computed-not-stored) lights up the confirmed/conflict states. Which slot a caller may fill derives from `product_vendors` ownership, never from the request — the AECI-520 `vendor_id`-scoping invariant extended to two slots.
- **Agreement/conflict surfacing + notification pipeline** (AECI-302) — **shipped 2026-08-17; §4 (surfacing) + §7 (pipeline) + §7.5.** The red vendor-vs-vendor conflict badge the AECi baseline can never trigger, plus the detector-fed notification pipeline. **Email-only at launch** (Resend + cron detectors + an in-portal list); in-portal live delivery lands in AECI-516 — **transport resolved 2026-08-19** as scoped revalidation, not sockets (§2.3 / §8.6 / ADR 0023 / `STAGE_2_REALTIME_SPEC.md` §6.2), and **email stays primary**. Nothing in the attestations epic waited on it, and nothing it shipped changes. **Four detectors shipped** — `silent-counterparty`, `open-conflict`, `stale-version`, `aeci-denied`. A fifth, `cross-grain`, was **dropped at build** (`STAGE_2_ATTESTATIONS_SPEC.md` §7.1 / §11): no doc in this repo ever defined it, and the only definition proposed describes legitimate data, since two mechanisms genuinely can move the same `data_object` in opposite directions.
- **Version-diff timeline** (AECI-303) — **shipped 2026-08-18; §9 + §9.4.** Per-product version selectors, built over the real `product_versions` entity migration 2 added (§8), because the dormant `introduced_at`/`deprecated_at` date stamps alone cannot express "source-version × target-version".
- **Paywalled integration depth** (AECI-304) — see §2.2; the diff is paywalled, the latest view is not. This epic ships the **seam**, not the gate.
- **Connector-powered edges are not attestable** (AECI-705) — **shipped 2026-08-31; the contract is `STAGE_2_ATTESTATIONS_SPEC.md` §14.** The `vendor_a`/`vendor_b` spine presumes two accountable *endpoint* vendors; on the 14% of edges a connector delivers, neither built the plumbing and the connector has no seat (§8.8(5)). So `POST /api/vendor/claims` and `PUT …/attestation` answer **403** on those edges, every vendor-addressed detector finding on them is dropped, and the portal renders them read-only with the connector named. **`DELETE` stays open** (an edge can *become* powered after a vendor attests) and the **ops-routed** findings survive (they are AECi's correction signal on its own curation). The predicate is a **union** — `powered_by_product_id IS NOT NULL OR mechanism_kind = 'iPaaS'` — because the two axes are independent and nothing cross-validates them: FK-only misses 53 production Zapier/Workato/n8n/Make/Boomi edges whose connector is not promoted, `iPaaS`-only misses 18 whose FK resolves to a connector-role product. A `powered_by`-vendor seat stays **out of scope**, owned by AECI-704.
- **Maintenance marker: real `last_reviewed_at` + vendor-maintained branch** (AECI-616) — **shipped 2026-08-18; the contract is `STAGE_2_ATTESTATIONS_SPEC.md` §13.** Stage 1 shipped the marker on `main` as attribution only (`Maintained by AEC Integrations` on product detail, vendor detail, and the pair page — a label, not a sentence, so no terminal period, and the date clause joins with a middot). The date clause and the `Vendor-maintained` branch existed in the component but were dormant, because **no column stored a real review timestamp**. Stage 2 adds `last_reviewed_at` + `maintained_by` to `vendors` / `products` / `integrations` (**migration 3**, `0018`), gates the write on an explicit `lastReviewedAt` signal in the promote payload — where **absence means untouched** — and lights up the vendor branch off real attestations. **Hard constraint: never source the date from `updated_at` / `created_at` / `promoted_at`, and never backfill it** — `updated_at` is `$onUpdate` and the promote ingest restamps it on every re-promote, so a bulk sweep would silently refresh the whole catalog's "freshness" without anyone re-checking a record.

> **Correction (2026-08-14 epic review — AECI-514): the "no migration" claim above was wrong.** It held for the *agreement engine* (computed-not-stored, ADR 0018) and for the `vendor_a`/`vendor_b` sources, and that much is unchanged. But two kickoff decisions (§8.4(1) vendor-created claims, §8.4(3) the product-version model) each require schema, so this epic ships **two additive migrations** — **three as of AECI-616**, which adds the maintenance-marker columns. They are specified in `STAGE_2_ATTESTATIONS_SPEC.md` §1.2. Do not plan against the old promise.

> **Two gaps found at the AECI-514 epic review (2026-08-14).** The table above is accurate about the *columns*, but readiness ≠ sufficiency:
>
> 1. **The version stamps are dates, not versions.** `introduced_at` / `deprecated_at` are ISO dates on an attestation. AECI-303's "source-version × target-version selectors" needs a **version entity per product**, and there is none in `schema.ts`. That is migration 2 of `STAGE_2_ATTESTATIONS_SPEC.md` §8.
> 2. **`computeAgreement` renders a *single* vendor's affirmation as `confirmed`.** ✅ **Closed by AECI-605** (2026-08-14). A `single_source` state now carries the one-sided case, rendered neutral and attributed ("Confirmed by {vendor}") with the counterparty's silence stated; `confirmed` requires two **distinct** vendor identities. Shipped ahead of the authoring API (AECI-301), which is what would first make the branch reachable — see the §1.1 release gate and §4.5 in `STAGE_2_ATTESTATIONS_SPEC.md`.
>
> A third finding is a live defect rather than a readiness gap: **`POST /api/promote` deletes claims by `integration_id` and cascades to attestations**, so the first re-promote of a claimed product would silently destroy every vendor attestation. Fixed in `STAGE_2_ATTESTATIONS_SPEC.md` §3.

## 4. Stack drift since §18 (AECI-282 AC #4)

§18 was written before the mid-Stage-1 platform changes. These are the points where its prose is stale and Stage 2 must plan against current reality:

1. **DB & authz — D1/Drizzle replaced Prisma/Supabase-PG (ADR 0016, AECI-278).** §18 implicitly assumes **Postgres RLS** for vendor-scoped access. **There is no RLS on app tables.** Stage 2 vendor authorization is the **3-layer Worker model** (`docs/AUTH_AND_RLS.md`): verify JWT → check `role`/`vendor_id`/`banned_at` → scope every Drizzle query by `vendor_id`. Multi-statement vendor writes use `db.batch([...])` (**D1 has no interactive transactions**) and must include their `audit_log` row in the same batch (§26.1).

2. **Cache invalidation — §18's `invalidateForEntity` reference is stale.** It was superseded by Cache-Tag purge (ADR 0004) and is now being re-migrated to **native Workers Cache + a cross-Worker Queue purge** (the in-flight **AECI-314** epic, ADR 0020 — see §6). Stage 2 vendor writes purge through whatever AECI-314 lands, via `Cache-Tag`, not a per-entity helper. Do not re-introduce `invalidateForEntity`.

3. **Email — Resend replaced Loops (AECI-240).** Every Stage 2 notification (claim approved/denied, conflict alerts, billing receipts) sends through the Resend client `apps/api/src/lib/email.ts` (`docs/email.md`), fail-open, not Loops.

4. **Auth — Supabase is Auth-only (ADR 0017).** One shared Supabase project across all environments; magic link + Google OAuth. `vendor_admin` provisioning is an **app-layer seam** (no `auth.users`↔`profiles` FK, AECI-254) — identical to how the admin role is granted. GDPR erasure remains an app-layer seam (AECI-254).

5. ~~**Real-time — Cloudflare Durable Objects, not a socket server.**~~ **Corrected 2026-08-19 (§8.6 / ADR 0023).** This bullet read §18's "WebSockets/SSE" and concluded that on Workers it is realized with Durable Objects (WebSocket hibernation). That was a translation of §18's assumption, not a decision, and it was **wrong about the destination**: the portal ships **no persistent socket at all**. The drift point that actually survives is narrower and still worth stating — *if* AECi ever needs push, the Workers-native answer is a Durable Object and not a standalone socket server, which is why ADR 0023's re-open trigger names DO WebSockets specifically. See §2.3.

6. **Read scaling — D1 Sessions API (AECI-250).** Read-replication is wired but inert until per-DB `read_replication: { mode: auto }` is flipped (ops action). A read-heavy vendor portal is the workload that would justify enabling it.

Explicit Stage-1 punts that belong to Stage 2 (the "Deferrals & Carryover" epic, AECI-518 — §7).

## 8. Decisions

Resolved **2026-07-12** (AECI-282 kickoff, Chris). The Stage-2-launch model is **concierge / manual onboarding** for the first vendor cohort: AECi reviews each claim by hand, arranges payment offline, and toggles verification manually. It deliberately does not scale — vendor volume at launch is low, and staffing help comes in if it grows.

### 8.1 Decided

1. **Verification bar — manual, reviewer-assisted.** AECi grants every `vendor_admin` by hand; **no auto-grant** at launch. The admin claim-review surface must **present verification signals** to the reviewer — email-domain match against the vendor's known domain(s), an email→LinkedIn/person lookup, and any other enrichment that helps confirm the claimant is who they say. Doesn't scale; acceptable — bring in help if volume warrants. (Gates AECI-513.)
2. **Seat model — multi-seat, flat.** Many `profiles` → one `vendor_id` (already schema-native, **zero migration**). A large vendor (Autodesk, Deltek…) will have several admins, not one. Every seat is individually granted through the §8.1(1) review, so multi-seat stays safe. Self-serve invite/revoke + an owner/admin distinction are **deferred** (need a small schema add). (Gates AECI-513.)
3. **Verification is a paid gate.** Becoming a **Verified** vendor carries a fee — modest, but real. **§8.8 carves out the connector surface** — a pure connector vendor is not invoiced for it, and **§8.9 settles what it receives instead: a catalogue-maintenance seat, never Verified.** Nothing here grants Verified for a catalogue feed. The free/default state is the **unclaimed, AECi-curated baseline** that renders "Unverified" (the Stage 1.5 status quo); **Verified (paid)** unlocks claim, data correction, integration attestation, the verified badge, richer profile fields, vendor analytics, and version-diff depth. This does **not** break the reader-facing trust model — that invariant is §8.1(4), and it is about the *reader*, not the vendor's cost to participate.
   - *Residual risk to watch:* if only paying vendors attest, attestation coverage skews toward payers. Mitigated by (a) the AECi-curated baseline staying **free to read**, and (b) AECI-304's invariant that **one-sided states are visibly labeled** — absence of a vendor attestation is never rendered as agreement. Tier structure/pricing above the entry Verified fee is **still open** (§8.2).
4. **Payer — vendors pay, always.** AECi's customer is the vendor. Viewer-pays tooling is possible far later, out of scope now. **Which vendor pays is refined by §8.8 (AECI-702, 2026-08-30): the *endpoint* vendor. A connector surface is not invoiced — a differentiation *within* vendor-pays, not a step toward viewer-pays, and the reason the model-agnostic clause below was written. What a pure connector vendor receives in return is settled by §8.9 (AECI-704, 2026-08-31): a catalogue-maintenance seat on its own catalogue(s), carried by no `vendor_entitlements` row at all.** Reader-facing invariants (AECI-304) hold: the latest-version view **and** the latest conflict / single-source state are always free and full-fidelity to readers; only the historical *diff depth* is gateable; one-sided (paid-vs-unpaid) states are labeled. Keep the entitlement flag model-agnostic so the payer model could swing later without a migration.
5. **Billing — offline invoicing / PO at launch.** No payment-provider integration in the first slice; larger vendors pay by **PO/invoice, not a credit card**. The system needs an **admin-toggled entitlement/verification state** + basic invoice/arrangement tracking (notices over Resend, §4). Automated billing (Stripe or similar) and self-serve card payment are **deferred** — start with the minimum.

### 8.2 Still open (further discussion — do not block AECI-513 on these)

- ~~**Tier ladder above the entry Verified fee**~~ — **structure resolved 2026-08-14, see §8.5(2).** The ladder is binary at launch and adding a rung is now a data-only change. *Pricing* above the entry fee remains a business question, not a build blocker.
- ~~**Offline-invoicing mechanics**~~ — **resolved 2026-08-14, see §8.5.** Who toggles, what is recorded, and the renewal/expiry posture are all settled; dunning stays deliberately out.
- ~~**Real-time transport** (§2.3)~~ — **resolved 2026-08-19, see §8.6.** Scoped client revalidation over a per-vendor freshness cursor; **no Durable-Object WebSockets, no SSE**, with a named re-open trigger (ADR 0023). The "until a concrete latency need proves one" condition held exactly as written: the enumeration found no sub-second need. (Search-index freshness *within* the portal was separately decided — §8.3(5): nightly ≤24h + immediate SSR — and never waited on this.)

1. ~~**Entitlement launch shape.**~~ **SUPERSEDED by §8.5(1) (2026-08-14).** As decided here: `vendors.verified` **is** the launch entitlement bit (per §8.1(5)); the PO/invoice arrangement is recorded in `audit_log` metadata; **no new schema** in the AECI-513 epic — the Paid Tiers epic (AECI-515) formalizes the entitlement model later. ### 8.4 Decided at build kickoff (2026-08-14 epic review — AECI-514)

Resolved when the Integration Attestations epic was decomposed. These **promote the epic's working decisions into the spec** and are the contract carried in `docs/STAGE_2_ATTESTATIONS_SPEC.md`; they refine §2.4 without contradicting §8.1.

1. **Vendors may CREATE claims, not just attest to AECi-seeded ones.** A vendor knows its own integration surface better than AECi's curation does; attest-only would cap coverage at whatever AECi happened to seed. Cost: a **provenance column on `claims`** (`origin` + `created_by_vendor_id`) and the promote carve-out in (4) below. **This is migration 1** — it overturns §2.4's original "no migration" promise.
2. **Attestation authority derives from product ownership, never from the request.** `vendor_a` = a vendor with a `product_vendors` row on the integration's `source_product_id`; `vendor_b` = the same on `target_product_id`; owning **both** endpoints permits attesting both slots; owning **neither is a 404, not a 403** (the AECI-520 non-disclosure rule). This is the two-slot extension of the §8.3 `vendor_id`-scoping invariant.
   - **One live attestation per slot**, enforced by a partial unique index on `(claim_id, source) WHERE retracted_at IS NULL`. Supersession is **retract-then-insert**, never `UPDATE`, so history stays append-only for the version timeline.
   - **`retracted_at` is a new column.** `deprecated_at` is a *version stamp* (`STAGE_1_5_SPEC.md` §3.3), not a retraction — the shipped `attestations_active_idx` comment conflates the two and is corrected.
3. **A real product-version model is added** (`product_versions` + version FKs on `attestations`) so AECI-303's per-product selectors are buildable. **This is migration 2.** Ordering keys off an explicit `sort_key`, never off the label (version labels do not sort lexically) or `released_at` (nullable). Versions are **vendor-authored only** at launch; promote does not ingest them.
4. **Promote coexistence — replace-by-origin.** `POST /api/promote` replaces only `origin='aeci'` claims and `source='aeci'` attestations; vendor rows survive re-promote, and claims upsert by their identity index so ids stay stable. When AECi drops a claim that carries a live vendor attestation, it is **converted to `origin='vendor'`, not deleted** — AECi withdraws its curation, the vendor's assertion stands. This fixes a **live defect**: today promote deletes claims by `integration_id` and cascades to attestations.
5. **`confirmed` requires two *distinct vendor identities*.** A new **`single_source`** agreement state is added so one vendor's affirmation is never rendered as bilateral agreement — the structural form of §8.1(4)'s "one-sided states are visibly labeled". `conflict` remains the only red state, and the AECi-never-red rule is unchanged.
6. **Notifications ship email-only** (Resend) with cron-driven detectors and an in-portal list. Live in-portal delivery is AECI-516 — nothing in the attestations epic waited on it, and nothing it shipped changed. **Its transport resolved 2026-08-19 (§8.6):** scoped client revalidation, **not** sockets. That answer adds no second channel — the portal polls a cursor whose `notifications` scope reuses this pipeline's exact `audit_log` predicate and refetches the same endpoint — and **email stays primary**. Note the direction of the dependency: this decision's daily cron cadence is precisely what made a socket indefensible in §8.6(1).
7. **Notification dedupe uses `audit_log` as the ledger** (`action: 'notification.sent'`), so the epic needs no notifications table and the in-portal list gets its backing query for free.
8. **The paywall is a seam, not a gate, in this epic.** AECI-303 shipped `canViewVersionDiff(...)` defaulting to open, in **`packages/shared/src/version-diff.ts`** (reached as `@aeci/shared/version-diff` — deliberately NOT `entitlements.ts`, which `aeci-515` already owns and which already declares the `'integration.version_diff'` capability id). Its single `apps/api` consult site is `resolveDiffAccess` in `apps/api/src/lib/pair-version-diff.ts`; the other is the web pair resolver. **AECI-304 swapped the implementation on 2026-08-19, and both of §9.4's ⚠️ notes are now resolved.** The gate is on **the pair's endpoint vendors, never the reader** — a reader never pays and is never identified — and because the pair's vendors are a function of the two slugs in the URL, the gate stayed **URL-derived**, so the edge-cache constraint was discharged by construction rather than by any of the three escape routes that note listed. The §8.1(4) invariant holds throughout: the **latest-version view and the latest conflict / single-source state are always free and full-fidelity**; only historical diff depth is gateable.

### 8.5 Decided at the Paid-Tiers epic review (2026-08-14 — AECI-515)

Resolved when the Paid Tiers epic was decomposed. These close §8.2's first two items and are the contract carried in **`docs/STAGE_2_PAID_TIERS_SPEC.md`**; they refine §8.1 and supersede §8.3(1) without contradicting the rest.

1. **Entitlement storage — a real table, with `vendors.verified` demoted to a mirror.** A new `vendor_entitlements` table (tier / status / term / offline-arrangement record) carries the model; **`vendors.verified` becomes a denormalized mirror**, maintained in the same `db.batch([...])`, with the invariant *`verified = true` **iff** an `active` entitlement row exists*. `vendor_id` is **UNIQUE** — that is what lets the mirror flip be a guarded single-row `UPDATE`, which is the only concurrency-safe shape available given D1 has no interactive transactions. **This supersedes §8.3(1)'s "no new schema"**, which was the AECI-513 launch shape and named AECI-515 as its successor. One migration, `0024_easy_sandman` (generated as `0006_*`, renumbered in AECI-622 — see `migrations.md` §0); everything downstream is additive-with-no-DDL. All five existing readers of `verified` (the public `?verified=` filter, `VendorLinkSchema`, `VendorDetail`, the Algolia vendor record, `aec-verified-badge`) are **untouched**.
2. **Tier ladder — binary at launch, extensible as data.** `unclaimed` (the AECi-curated baseline) vs `verified` (the paid entry fee). A **capability registry** (`packages/shared/src/entitlements.ts`, pure data, zod-free) maps tier → capability set, so adding a rung later is a data edit and not a code change. `tier` is deliberately **unconstrained** at the DB layer for exactly that reason (`workflow_instances_type_check` is the cautionary precedent); an unknown tier fails closed to zero capabilities.
3. **Renewal / expiry — warn, never auto-lapse.** Entitlements carry `period_start` / `period_end`. A daily cron **warns** the vendor's seats and `ADMIN_ALERT_EMAIL` before expiry and **never writes `status`**. Auto-lapse would strip a badge from a paying customer over a data-entry mistake; **un-verifying stays a deliberate admin action** — consistent with §8.3(2). No dunning.
4. **Who toggles — an admin, through the entitlement, never the bit.** `PATCH /api/admin/vendors/:id/entitlement` (`set` / `renew` / `clear`) is the sole owner of the un-verify half §8.3(2) left unowned. `verified` is never named in the payload; it follows in the same batch. **Clearing an entitlement does not revoke seats** — the vendor keeps portal access, read-only, with a renewal notice.
5. **No pay-for-placement becomes an asserted property.** The entitlement capability vocabulary and the Algolia ranking vocabulary (`searchableAttributes ∪ attributesForFaceting ∪ customRanking` across `INDEX_SETTINGS`) are proven **disjoint** by unit test, and no cacheable SSR component may import the entitlement registry. The invariant stops depending on reviewer vigilance.
6. ~~**AECI-304 stays parented to AECI-515 but is excluded from the epic's build order and completion criteria**~~ — it was blocked by the then-unbuilt AECI-303. **Both shipped 2026-08-18/19**, so 304 landed on the epic branch after all. Minting the `integration.version_diff` capability id up front did what it was meant to: 304 needed no registry edit, only the two consult sites. It is **not** a render-path gate on a component, though — it is a gate on the **pair's vendor tiers**, in `canViewVersionDiff` (`STAGE_2_PAID_TIERS_SPEC.md` §11).

### 8.6 Decided at the Real-Time epic review (2026-08-19 — AECI-516)

Resolved when the Real-Time / Live Portal epic was decomposed. These **close §8.2's third and last open item** and are the contract carried in **`docs/STAGE_2_REALTIME_SPEC.md`**; the transport decision itself is **ADR 0023**. They refine §2.3 and correct §4(5) without contradicting §8.1.

1. **Transport — scoped client revalidation, not sockets.** The portal goes live through a cheap per-vendor **freshness cursor** that the browser polls; **Durable-Object WebSockets and SSE are declined.** The deciding move was to enumerate every event that can change a vendor's portal state and ask what latency each actually needs: **none is sub-second.** The vendor's own edit needs **zero transport** (the `PATCH`/`PUT` echo already carries post-edit state); **two of the six event classes are produced by a daily cron** (`ATTESTATION_NOTIFY_CRON` 10:00, `ENTITLEMENT_EXPIRY_CRON` 11:00), so a socket would deliver a 24-hour-stale event with 50 ms of transport latency; and the one genuine deadline — the concierge entitlement toggle (§8.5(4)), where an admin is often on the phone with the vendor — is **≤ ~1 min**. Against that, a DO costs a `durable_objects` binding plus a `migrations` block in **four** wrangler environments, a WebSocket upgrade threaded through the SSR Worker's `/api/*` service-binding passthrough, fan-out coupling on every `/api/vendor/*` write plus the admin entitlement action and two cron consumers, and a new failure surface with no local emulation. SSE without a DO is strictly worse — it holds a Worker invocation open **and** still polls D1 inside it. The house pattern for "the server did something, tell the client" already exists: ADR 0021's `GET /api/promote/jobs/:id`.
   - **This is a dated decision with a re-open trigger, not a permanent no.** Adopt DO WebSockets when **any** of: the detectors move off the daily cron to write-triggered (sub-minute) evaluation; multi-seat concurrent editing produces a **measured** lost-update / stale-overwrite rate; or measured `aeci.api.vendor.updates` volume at the shipped cadence exceeds the cost of a hibernating DO. Two of the three are measurable off metrics this epic ships.
2. **The cursor is per-scope, not one opaque revision.** `GET /api/vendor/updates` returns a revision map over six scopes (`profile` · `entitlement` · `products` · `integrations` · `notifications` · `requests`) plus `server_time`, computed as **six SELECTs in one `db.batch`** — one D1 round trip — and the client refetches **only** what moved. An opaque single token would turn any counterparty attestation into a three-call full-dashboard refetch, on the interval, forever.
   - **Invariant: every cursor query reuses the scoping predicate of the handler it is a cursor for.** A divergent cursor fails silently in one of two directions — too narrow and the section is permanently stale with no error anywhere; too wide and a timestamp **leaks the existence of another vendor's row**, which is the AECI-520 non-disclosure rule failing through a side channel.
   - The endpoint is a **pure read**: no `audit_log` row (§26.1 governs writes, and `audit_log` is also the §8.4(7) notification ledger the portal queries), `private, no-store`, and **not** entitlement-gated — reads never are, or a lapsed vendor would be 404'd out of the renewal notice they are being billed for.
3. **No schema, no infrastructure.** **Zero migrations** — the cursor reports the age of state that already exists rather than storing a new counter, deliberately, because a `vendor_revisions` table would have to be maintained by every writer across three epics: the same fan-out coupling the socket was declined for. No binding, no queue, no provisioning step. This is the contrast with §8.5(1) (one migration) and §8.4 (three).
4. **Optimistic for toggles, pessimistic for forms.** Assert / deny / retract apply locally and **roll back with a visible error**; profile and product edits keep their echo-reconcile. **Showing "Saved" before it saved is a worse lie than a 300 ms wait**, and a form has too many partially-valid intermediate states to guess at. Recorded as a decision so a later reviewer does not "finish the job".
5. **A background refresh never clobbers an in-flight edit.** A section holding unsaved changes **defers** reconciliation and surfaces a non-blocking "Updated elsewhere — reload this section" affordance. Implemented once in the shared store, not six times in six components.
6. **Notifications surface as a session-scoped count, never a banner.** The rows are a historical record of an **emailed** nudge over a 90-day window; promoted to a banner, a three-week-old "Vendors disagree" row would sit above a lane that now reads `confirmed` and the surface would visibly contradict itself. This is the in-portal half of §8.4(6), and email stays primary.
7. **One polite live region on `/vendor`, hoisted — never a second.** The surface already ships exactly one (then in `vendor-integrations-section.ts`, part of the `/vendor` axe contract); it moves up to the dashboard shell. Two live regions on one page make announcements race and duplicate. Never `assertive`, never focus-stealing, never a layout shift under the pointer. *(As built 2026-08-19: the region is fed by a dedicated root service, **`VendorPortalAnnouncer`**, not from the store — once the region is in the shell, a control five levels down needs a channel, and announcement copy is presentation rather than portal data. Both dashboard concepts host one region, and only one concept renders at a time. `STAGE_2_REALTIME_SPEC.md` §6.3 / §6.5, including the open finding that four **conditional** `role="status"` elements survive elsewhere in the tree.)*

### 8.7 Decided at the Deferrals epic review (2026-08-20 — AECI-518)

Resolved when the Stage-1 Deferrals & Carryover epic was reviewed. These close the **`STAGE_1_PHASE_2_SPEC.md` §9.2 integration-JSON-LD deferral** and record why the other two §5 bullets ship no code. Unlike §8.3–§8.6 there is no companion build spec — the epic is small enough that §5 + this subsection *are* the contract.

1. **Integration structured data is `WebPage` + `about`, not a bespoke type.** §9.2 deferred on "no clean schema.org type exists", and that observation was right: schema.org has no *integration* type, and the page is not itself a `SoftwareApplication`. The honest model is a **`schema.org/WebPage`** whose `about` array names the two endpoint products, each as a nested `SoftwareApplication` carrying `@id` / `name` / `url` and — when present — `applicationCategory`, `image`, and an `Organization` `publisher`. `about` is *"the subject matter of the content"*, which is exactly true here.
   - **The `@id` is the point.** Each `about` node's `@id` is the URI the product's own detail page publishes on its `SoftwareApplication` block (AECI-518 added it there for this reason), so the two describe **one entity** and the site reads as a graph rather than as isolated islands. Both sides compose it through the single `productLdId(origin, slug)` helper — a hand-built string on either side would silently produce two unrelated nodes and nothing would fail.
   - **`url` on an `about` node is the AECi product page, not the vendor site** — unlike the product detail page's own block, which uses `product.website`. `ProductListItem` (the pair payload's endpoint shape) carries no `website`, and the AECi URL is the better target regardless: it is where the referenced node is actually published.
2. **A `BreadcrumbList` ships alongside it**, mirroring the trail the pair page already renders (`Home › {context} › {other}`), because it is the only block on this page a search engine renders as a rich result. Google requires breadcrumb markup to match visible content, so the template dictates the shape — including the final crumb being the *other* product's name rather than the pair title. That crumb omits `item` per convention for the current page, which also keeps the payload honest on the non-default orientation, whose canonical points elsewhere. **This is pair-page scope only**; generalizing breadcrumbs site-wide is a separate, untracked opportunity.
3. **Rejected alternatives**, recorded so they are not re-proposed:
   - **`ItemList` of the mechanisms** — `ItemList` yields a carousel only for a fixed set of eligible types, and `SoftwareApplication` is not among them. Inert markup with a real maintenance cost.
   - **`SoftwareApplication` as the page's main entity** — the page is not an application. False markup.
   - **`FAQPage`** — Google restricted FAQ rich results to government/health sites in 2023, and synthesising Q&A the page does not visibly render is precisely the gaming the trust-first positioning (§1, §8.1(4)) rules out.
4. **Structured data rides the page's existing `noindex` decision.** Emission is gated on the *same* single condition the resolver already computes — an empty pair (thin content) or a non-default version selection (§9.2 of `STAGE_1_5_SPEC.md`'s addendum) suppresses both the index and the LD. Describing a page we are telling crawlers to skip is at best inert and at worst a mismatch signal.
5. **Forward-compatible with AECI-340 by construction.** `STAGE_1_5_SPEC.md` §11.2 will give each orientation a self-referential canonical. Because the payload is a pure function of `(pair, canonical, origin)` and `canonical` is already the resolver's single source of truth, that flip needs **no change** to any of this.
   - **The payload is framed by the URL, the `url`/`@id` by the canonical — and until AECI-340 those differ on the non-default orientation.** Visiting `/products/revit/integrations/procore` today emits `name: "Revit and Procore integrations"` and `about: [Revit, Procore]` while `url` reports the alphabetical canonical (`/products/procore/…`). That is **deliberate, not an oversight.** The alternative — reordering the payload to the canonical's frame — would put the LD's `name` at odds with the page's own `<title>` (which is URL-framed), forfeiting the property that makes drift structurally impossible, and it would put the `BreadcrumbList` at odds with the visible trail, which Google requires it to match. The exposure is negligible either way: both orientations are *about* the same two products, so only ordering differs, and Google prefers the canonical page's own markup, which is self-consistent. **AECI-340 dissolves the mismatch entirely** by making each orientation self-canonical.
6. **`MetaService` now clears JSON-LD on every head-owning entry point.** Not a new feature — the fix for a live defect this epic would otherwise have compounded. Before AECI-518 only `setNotFoundMeta` / `setSearchMeta` purged, so an in-app navigation off a product or vendor detail page carried that page's block onto the pair, taxonomy-browse, `/about`, and `/legal/*` pages, all of which set meta but publish no LD of their own. SSR was always clean (a fresh per-URL app), so this only ever misdescribed the client-navigated SPA — which is still what a JS-rendering crawler sees. One `clearJsonLd()` replaces three duplicated loops.

### 8.8 Decided at the connector-lane session (2026-08-28 — AECI-702), confirmed against the tier model 2026-08-30

Resolved when the connector lane (epic AECI-695) reached its commercial question, and confirmed against this section before Stage 2 launch as the session required. These **refine §8.1(3) and §8.1(4); they reverse neither** — §8.1(4)'s hard prohibition is on *viewer*-pays, and this is a differentiation *within* vendor-pays, which is what that bullet's closing sentence reserved room for. They gate **AECI-704**. There is no companion build spec, because **the decision needs no code and no migration.**

**Scope discipline: this settles who is billed, and nothing else.** Payment terms, tiers, and entitlement mechanics are not being worked yet, so (2) deliberately leaves the return side of the trade open rather than inventing an answer the rest of the model would then have to honour.

1. **The payer is the endpoint vendor; the connector surface is not invoiced.** AECi's paying customer is the vendor whose product sits at an *endpoint* of an integration. A pure **connector** product — `products.product_role = 'connector'` — carries no fee of its own.
   - **The hybrid rule: `hybrid` counts as endpoint.** A `hybrid` product *is* an endpoint as well as a connector, so it is chargeable on its endpoint side; its connector function adds no fee and confers no exemption. The payer test is therefore **"does this vendor own any product with an endpoint surface — `product_role IN ('application','hybrid')`?"** If yes it is an ordinary paying vendor. Only a vendor **all** of whose products are `'connector'` is a *pure* connector vendor.
   - **Scoped per product surface, never per vendor.** `product_role` is a per-product column and `vendors` carries **no connector marker at all** (no `role`/`kind`/`is_connector`; a "connector vendor" is only derivable through `product_vendors`). A per-vendor exemption would therefore be both unimplementable and wrong: **Autodesk, Trimble, Deltek and Sage Group each own connector-role products** and are among the largest endpoint accounts in the catalogue. Exempting them is the opposite of the intent.
   - **It costs nothing to enforce.** Entitlement is vendor-level (`vendor_entitlements.vendor_id` is UNIQUE, §8.5(1)) and there is no per-product pricing, so a mixed vendor already buys one entitlement covering every product it owns and its connector products ride along by construction.
2. **What a pure connector vendor receives *in return* for its catalogue feed was deliberately left OPEN here — owned by AECI-704.** **CLOSED by §8.9 (2026-08-31);** the reasoning below is kept because §8.9 rests on it. The confirmed half was the one above: they are not billed. The return side was not decided here, and was **not to be inferred** from the fact that the feed is described as being taken "instead" of a fee.
   - **The candidate answer already on the table** was AECI-704's 2026-08-29 revision: a **catalog-maintenance seat** on the AECi connector admin page (stubs, mappings, per-app depth on the vendor's own catalogue) and explicitly **not** the Verified badge, attestation seats, or any ranking-relevant surface. That line stood, and **§8.9(1) adopted it verbatim.**
   - **A constraint discovered while confirming, which will bind whoever closes this:** `vendors.verified` mirrors off **`status = 'active'`, not off `tier`** (`apps/api/src/lib/vendor-entitlement.ts` — the module-header mirror invariant and the `ACTIVE` constant, *"the one status that mirrors onto `vendors.verified`"*; there is no `MIRRORING_STATUS` symbol, as an earlier draft of this bullet claimed). So **any** active `vendor_entitlements` row lights the verified badge, whatever its tier. "A seat but no badge" is therefore **not expressible through the entitlement table** as built; it needs its own surface (AECI-722 / AECI-724), or a deliberate change to the mirror. Adding a `partner` tier would *not* achieve it. **§8.9(2) took the first branch:** the seat is not an entitlement row at all, so the mirror is left untouched.
   - **A second constraint, if the return side ever does become an entitlement row:** give it a **term**, never a perpetual one. *(§8.9(2) decided it does not become one — so there is no term to give; §8.9(4) rehomes the re-confirmation duty this bullet exists to protect.)* `vendor_entitlements_expiry_idx` is partial (`WHERE period_end IS NOT NULL AND status = 'active'`), so a perpetual row is structurally invisible to the daily expiry cron (`STAGE_2_PAID_TIERS_SPEC.md` §7) and nobody would ever be prompted to check the feed is still arriving. §8.5(3) still governs either way: the sweep warns and never writes `status`.
   - **Nothing is lost by waiting.** The tracked catalogues are `managed_by: review` today, so a connector vendor needs no portal capability to keep its feed flowing, and granting a badge is a one-way door — withdrawing one later costs more than never granting it.
3. **Whatever the carve-out ends up covering, it is the entry Verified fee — not the ladder above it.** Anything on the §8.2 ladder (still an open *pricing* question) is sold to connector vendors on the same terms as anyone else: a connector that wants enhanced profile features **to help it sell the connector** buys them like any other vendor. `STAGE_2_PAID_TIERS_SPEC.md` §3.1 left `profile.rich_fields` unassigned for exactly this kind of reason — "pre-assigning fields to a rung nobody has priced would bake in an answer."
4. **Why — and what the confirmation actually established.** The position rests on three legs, and the session recorded that it "rests on structure; current pricing and whether endpoint vendors pay today were not known." Both unknowns are now closed:
   - *Integrity.* The connector lane is where capability gets sold as delivery (AECI-670's whole point). A connector vendor as paying customer argues about thousands of edges; an endpoint vendor argues about its own.
   - *Arithmetic and supply,* measured against the review catalogue on **2026-08-30**: **308 of 2,428** integration edges are `mechanism_kind='iPaaS'` (**12.7%** — the "13% of the catalogue exists only because of connectors"), leaving **2,120** direct edges; **86** connector-role products of which **8 `promoted` + 2 `on_hold`** are the ten in play; against **1,528 products / 1,003 vendors** in the pipeline. Scale of a single feed: MindCloud's catalogue alone is **3,395 listings** with per-listing action counts — larger than AECi's whole product catalogue and edge set combined. **These are pipeline-side figures and are dated deliberately** — the live catalogue is far smaller, no such count is recorded anywhere else in the repo, and the *ratio* is the durable claim, not the absolute numbers.
   - *No pricing conflict exists to resolve.* The entry Verified fee has **never been assigned a number** anywhere in this repo — "modest, but real" (§8.1(3)) is the whole of it, and §8.2 leaves pricing explicitly open. The `"USD 5,000 / yr"` literal that appears in the docs and in `apps/api/seed/*.sql` is a **format example and seed fixture**, not a decision. And no vendor pays *today*: §8.1(5) billing is offline PO/invoice with no payment provider (Stripe is a Stage 4 item per `CICD_PLAN.md` §13.3).
   - *The model already implements the payer half.* AECI-304's paywall gates on **the pair's two endpoint vendors, never the connector** (`packages/shared/src/version-diff.ts`, `canViewVersionDiff`), and §8.4(2) gives a vendor owning neither endpoint a **404** on attestation. So a connector vendor that bought Verified today could not attest to a single edge it powers — **there is currently almost nothing to sell one.** The carve-out ratifies a shipped position rather than introducing a new one.
5. **Known consequence for whoever closes (2) — `attestation.author` would be inert for connectors.** *(Doubly moot now: §8.9(1)/(2) grant a connector seat no attestation capability at all, and AECI-705 removed the endpoint seat on these edges from the other side.)* Any grant that carried the capability would not confer working access: §8.4(2) derives attestation authority from `product_vendors` on the edge's `source_product_id` / `target_product_id` **only**, so a connector vendor owning neither endpoint still 404s on the edges it powers.
   - **AECI-705 CLOSED it from the other side, 2026-08-31** (`STAGE_2_ATTESTATIONS_SPEC.md` §14). It did not give connectors a seat — it removed the *endpoint* vendors' seat on those edges, which is the honest reading of the same fact: on a connector-powered edge nobody with an attestation seat built the plumbing. `POST` / `PUT` now answer **403** there (not 404 — the caller has already proven it owns an endpoint, and powered-ness is public on the pair page), every vendor-addressed detector nudge is suppressed, and the portal explains rather than prompts. So the inertness is now **symmetric and deliberate** rather than an accident of the authority rule.
   - **The gate is `powered_by_product_id IS NOT NULL OR mechanism_kind = 'iPaaS'`**, which settles the axis mismatch this section flagged: measured on production 2026-08-31, FK-only selects 79 of 946 edges and `iPaaS`-only 114, overlapping on 61 — the union is **132 (14.0%)**, and each half alone misses a real population the other catches. It over-includes roughly ten edges an endpoint vendor genuinely built on an iPaaS, accepted because under-inclusion breaks the acceptance criterion while over-inclusion only costs coverage.
   - **What (2) still owes** is unchanged: a connector's own voice on the edges it delivers remains unbuilt and is AECI-704's, recorded in `STAGE_2_ATTESTATIONS_SPEC.md` §11. AECI-705 deliberately did not invent one.
6. **Also owned by AECI-704: the operator runbook note.** **LANDED 2026-08-31 — `STAGE_2_VENDOR_PORTAL_SPEC.md` §5.2, summarised in §8.9(6).** The concierge claim flow must **route** a connector-vendor claim to the partnership track rather than decline it: neither Grant nor Reject is correct, so the claim is parked `open` and the conversation happens out of band. Until AECI-722 / AECI-724 build that surface, the routing is a manual operator step.
7. **Deferred, and deliberately the opposite incentive: lead flow on *unserved* pairs.** Connectors may later pay for demand signal on pairs nobody delivers — **paid on the pair being undelivered**, which is the inverse of the incentive that would have them inflate delivery. A different product from this one; not built, not scoped, recorded so the incentive property is not lost.

### 8.9 Decided when the connector return side closed (2026-08-31 — AECI-704)

Resolved when AECI-704 took up the two things §8.8 deliberately deferred and named: **§8.8(2)** (what a pure connector vendor receives *in return* for its catalogue feed) and **§8.8(6)** (the operator runbook note). These **close both**; they refine nothing else in §8, and — like §8.8 — **need no code, no schema and no migration.** There is no companion build spec: the surfaces this decision fences are AECI-722's and AECI-724's to build, and this subsection is the fence they build inside.

1. **What a pure connector vendor receives is a catalogue-maintenance seat, and nothing else.** The seat carries **stubs, mappings, and per-app depth on that vendor's own catalogue(s)** — the AECI-722 screen, held by an AECI-724 vendor seat. It carries **no** Verified badge, **no** attestation seat, **no** profile/product edit on endpoint products, and **no** ranking-relevant surface of any kind. This confirms AECI-704's 2026-08-29 revision **unreversed**, exactly as §8.8(2) said it stood.

2. **The seat is NOT a `vendor_entitlements` row. This is a fence, not a build — no row, no tier, no capability id, no migration.**
   - **The constraint §8.8(2) flagged is real:** `vendors.verified` mirrors off **`status = 'active'`, not `tier`** (`apps/api/src/lib/vendor-entitlement.ts`), so *any* active row lights the badge and "seat but no badge" is not expressible through the entitlement table.
   - **But the decisive fact is stronger than the mirror: there is no path today that creates a vendor seat without opening an entitlement.** `approveClaim` composes `grantSeatStatements` with `activateEntitlementStatements` into one batch at `GRANT_TIER = 'verified'` (`apps/api/src/routes/admin-claims.ts`), and `ent.stmts` is empty *only* when the vendor already has an active row — so approving a connector vendor's claim always opens one. The seat-invite redeem path requires an existing **owner** seat, which only a grant creates. And no admin route writes `role = 'vendor_admin'` at all: `admin-users.ts` / `admin-vendors.ts` ban and unban existing seats.
   - **Both workarounds someone would reach for are already foreclosed by shipped code — and this subsection says so precisely because AECI-724 lands *after* Stage 2 launch in the lane order.** A `partner` tier cannot be granted: `apps/api/src/routes/admin-entitlements.ts` returns **403** on any `set` whose tier grants zero capabilities (*"clear the entitlement instead of granting it"*), and `SetVendorEntitlementSchema.tier` derives from `PAID_TIERS` rather than `TIERS`, so Zod rejects it first — and it would light the badge through the status mirror regardless. Approve-then-clear is rejected in (6).
   - **The existing primitive works unmodified.** `findVendorProfile` (`apps/api/src/lib/authz.ts`) uses a `leftJoin`, not an `innerJoin`, precisely so a vendor with **no** entitlement row still authenticates, still passes `requireVendor()`, and resolves to `unclaimed` with zero capabilities; `apps/api/src/routes/vendor-seat-invites.ts` records the matching rule that **seats are deliberately not entitlement-gated**. So the connector admin routes authorize on **`profiles.role = 'vendor_admin'` + `profiles.vendor_id`**, ownership-checked against a `connector`-role product — **never through `requireCapability`**, and with **no new capability id** (`CAPABILITIES` is frozen by `entitlements.spec.ts`, and a new id would need a tier to hold it, which would need a row, which would light the badge).

3. **Provisioning is the open sub-question, and it is now its own issue.** Binding the authorization primitive does not create the seat. Because no route writes `vendor_admin`, the only path that exists today is claim-approve — the thing (6) forbids. **AECI-722 shipped 2026-08-31 and deliberately did not close this**: its screen renders the lane and drives AECI-720's flip, which records who a catalogue was handed to and grants nothing (the endpoint says `seat_not_granted: true` in its own audit metadata). Provisioning went to **AECI-740, a separate issue rather than AECI-724**, on the timing this subsection itself notes: 724 lands *after* Stage 2 launch, while §5.2 is blocked *before* it — so the seat cannot wait for the portal that consumes it. This bullet's own parenthetical offered the shape ("or a small admin action"); that is the branch taken. Until it ships there is still **no way to hand a connector vendor its seat**, which is exactly why the runbook parks the claim rather than granting it.

4. **§8.8(2)'s "give it a term" goes moot — its purpose does not.** With no entitlement row there is no `period_end`, so the daily expiry cron (`STAGE_2_PAID_TIERS_SPEC.md` §7) has nothing to sweep: nothing drifts, and `entitlement_mirror_drift` stays clean. But nothing then ever asks *"is the feed still arriving?"* either. That re-confirmation duty moves to the connector lane — **AECI-720's `managed_by` cutoff, which SHIPPED 2026-08-31** as `PATCH /api/admin/connector-catalogs/:id` plus the promote-side refusal (`API_CONTRACTS.md` §6.10/§6.12; the flag is reversible precisely so this duty is actionable — a lane can be reclaimed when a feed stops) — plus catalogue freshness (`connector_catalog_surfaces.last_ingested_at` — the per-surface "as of" stamp AECI-714 landed AECi-side, alongside the per-stub `connector_stubs.actions_fetched_at`), reviewed on the connector admin screen. **That screen SHIPPED 2026-08-31 too** (AECI-722, `ADMIN_PANEL_SPEC.md` §5.9): the freshness stamp is its second section, above the triage queue, and the flip has a control. So this duty is now actually performable rather than merely assigned — which was the whole point of moving it here. Named here so the perpetual-and-unwatched failure §8.8(2) warned about does not simply reappear in a different table.

5. **A consequence handed to AECI-724: the `none` plan panel is an upsell, not lapsed copy.** `apps/web/src/app/vendor/components/vendor-plan-panel.ts` already distinguishes *never arranged* from *lapsed* across five states, so a zero-entitlement connector seat lands on **`none`** — which reads *"Your account is not verified yet… opens up once verification is active"* behind an **"Ask about verification"** call to action. For a connector seat that is an upsell for something the carve-out says they will never be sold. AECI-724's job is therefore to **suppress or replace** that panel for a connector seat, not to soften its copy.

6. **The operator runbook note (closing §8.8(6)): route, do not decline — and do not approve.** The full procedure is **`STAGE_2_VENDOR_PORTAL_SPEC.md` §5.2**; the three rules are: **Grant is wrong** (approve is unconditional — it opens a `verified` entitlement, flips the mirror and hands every capability in `TIER_CAPABILITIES.verified`; there is no partial grant); **Reject is also wrong** (`sendClaimRejectedEmail` states the claim "was not approved", the reviewer's `reason` is internal-only, and a decline is the one-way door §8.8(2) refused for the badge — the reasoning applies harder to an email aimed at exactly the party we want a relationship with); so the claim is **parked as `open`** and the routing conversation happens out of band. It stays a manual operator step until AECI-722 / AECI-724 exist.

7. **What this does NOT change.** §8.8(1)'s payer test is untouched. §8.8(3) still holds: anything on the §8.2 ladder above the entry fee is sold to connector vendors on the same terms as anyone else — the carve-out waives the entry fee, and this subsection gives away nothing that is priced. §8.8(5) becomes **hypothetical**: the seat carries no attestation capability at all, so no connector seat can reach the edges it powers, and **AECI-705 remains the sole owner** of gating attestation prompts off connector-powered edges. §8.8(7)'s deferred lead-flow product is unaffected.
